01

Lasp → Partisan → LoJack

From Lasp to LoJack’s vehicle-recovery platform.

PPDP 2025 · 10 Year Most Influential Paper Award
LaspModel
→
PartisanRuntime
→
LoJackProduction

I co-created Lasp with Peter Van Roy: a model for distributed programs that converge without coordinating every update. I implemented the Erlang prototype and scaled it to 1,024 Erlang nodes on AWS while contracting at Mesosphere.

To scale Lasp, I built the communication layer that became Partisan. LeapSight adopted it in LoJack LATAM’s vehicle-recovery platform, reported in 2022 at 300,000 vehicles and more than 30 million GPS transmissions a day. BarrelDB adopted it too. I also ported Riak Core to Partisan while consulting for LeapSight.

Filibuster → DoorDash → further research

Resilience testing shaped by real services.

FilibusterSystematic testing
→
DoorDashReal services
→
ResearchNew algorithms

I created Filibuster to systematically test how microservices handle failure. During my DoorDash internship, I adapted the algorithms for Kotlin/gRPC concurrency and built instrumentation that didn’t require application changes. I then tested DashPass as a contractor.

The industrial constraints fed back into the research: circuit breakers, distributed execution indexing, and database-resilience testing with students I mentored.
